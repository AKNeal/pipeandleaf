Grown in Alaska

Every gram, from up here.

Not a marketing angle — a legal fact. Cannabis can't cross state lines, so there's no such thing as an import in this industry. Everything on our shelf was grown, cured, baked or bottled somewhere in Alaska, usually by people we've met.

Why it's all local, whether we like it or not

Cannabis remains federally illegal, which makes it a crime to transport across a state border — including from Washington to Alaska, and including by mail, barge, or plane. Every legal state therefore runs a closed loop: grown in-state, processed in-state, tested in-state, sold in-state.

In most places that's a footnote. Up here it's the whole story. Alaska is one of the few states where "buy local" isn't a choice a retailer makes — it's the only thing physically and legally available. Which means when you buy a gram in Fairbanks, the money stays in a supply chain that starts a few hours' drive away at most.

What that means for you

Alaskan cannabis is grown almost entirely indoors — you cannot outdoor-farm a crop through an Interior winter — which makes it expensive to produce and generally very consistent. Alaska also has some of the strictest testing rules in the country. That's a real quality floor.

Fairbanks Thunderfuck, and the Alaskan strain legend

You'll spot Fairbanks Thunderfuck in our flower case from time to time, and the name is a nod to the most famous thing Alaska ever contributed to cannabis: Matanuska Thunderfuck — "MTF" — the legendary sativa-leaning strain associated with the Matanuska Valley north of Anchorage, and one of the few American strains with genuine mythology attached.

Strain lineage claims across the industry are notoriously hard to verify, and modern MTF-labeled genetics vary a lot from grower to grower. But the cultural point stands: Alaska has been growing serious cannabis since long before it was legal, largely because a place with this climate and this much isolation breeds people who figure out how to grow things indoors.

Storing it through an Interior winter

Cold is not a preservative here. A few Fairbanks-specific notes:

  • Don't leave it in the truck. At forty below, trichomes turn brittle and snap off with the slightest handling — that's your potency and flavor on the floor of the bag.
  • Don't freeze it. Same problem, plus freeze-thaw cycles pull in moisture, and moisture invites mold.
  • Watch the dry side too. Interior winter air is extremely dry, and heated indoor air is drier still. Flower that's over-dried gets harsh and loses aroma fast. An airtight glass jar solves most of this.
  • Cool, dark, sealed, room temperature. A cupboard away from the stove. That's the whole method.
