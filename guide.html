Beginner's Guide

Your first time, minus the guesswork.

Walking into a dispensary for the first time is a little like ordering coffee in a country where you don't speak the language. Here's the whole thing, start to finish, so you can skip the awkward part and get to the good part.

Before you come in

Bring your ID. You must be 21 or older, and we check every single time — first visit, hundredth visit, birthday suit, doesn't matter. A valid unexpired government photo ID: driver's license, state ID, passport, or military ID.

Bring cash if you can. Cannabis is still federally illegal, which means most banks won't touch dispensaries. Both our shops have an ATM on site if you forget, but cash saves you the fee.

You can't order ahead. No online ordering, no delivery, no curbside — Alaska requires the sale to happen in the store. Our live menu tells you what's on the shelf right now so you're not driving in blind.

The good news

You do not need to know anything before you arrive. Genuinely. "I've never done this and I don't know what I want" is one of our favorite sentences — it means we get to actually help instead of just ringing you up.

The single most important rule

Start low. Go slow. That's it. That's the rule. You can always take more in an hour; you cannot take less once it's in you.

Nearly every bad cannabis story you've ever heard — the friend who "had a brownie once and hated it" — comes down to one mistake: taking too much, too fast, usually with edibles, usually because nothing happened after twenty minutes so they had another.

Edibles are the one to respect

Edibles take 30 minutes to 2 hours to kick in, and the effect can keep climbing for hours after that. Take one dose. Then wait two full hours before even considering another. Set a timer if you have to.

What if I take too much?

It's unpleasant, but it passes. Nobody has ever died from a cannabis overdose. What you might get is anxiety, a racing heart, dizziness, nausea, or the deeply unfun sensation that time has stopped.

What actually helps: find somewhere quiet and comfortable, drink water, eat something plain, put on a show you've seen a hundred times, and remind yourself out loud that this is temporary and caused by a plant. Have someone sit with you if you can. Sleep it off if you can.

When it's more than that

If someone is having trouble breathing, can't be woken up, is vomiting repeatedly, or has chest pain, call 911. Cannabis is rarely the whole story in those cases, and it's not worth guessing. You will not get in trouble — it's legal here for adults 21+.

The ways you can take it

Flower

The actual bud, which you grind and smoke in a pipe, bong, or rolled paper. Kicks in within minutes, peaks around 30 minutes, mostly done in 2–3 hours. Easiest to control because you feel it fast. Hardest on your lungs.

Pre-rolls

Flower already ground and rolled for you. Same effects as flower, zero rolling skill required. Our 5-packs are a cheap way to try a few strains without committing to a whole jar.

Vape carts

Concentrated oil in a cartridge that screws onto a battery. Fast like smoking, far less smell, very portable. Potency is high — one short pull is a real dose.

Edibles

Gummies, chocolates, baked goods, drinks. Slow to start, long to last (4–8 hours, sometimes longer), and noticeably more of a full-body experience. The most beginner-friendly option if you respect the timing, and the most likely to go sideways if you don't.

Concentrates

Shatter, sugar, rosin, crumble — cannabis stripped down to its strongest parts, often 60–80% THC. Requires specific gear and a tolerance you don't have yet on day one. Worth exploring later.

Topicals

Balms and creams you rub on. These generally do not get you high — they're for localized aches. A perfectly good place to start if intoxication isn't what you're after.

Indica, sativa, hybrid — the honest version

You'll see these on every label, and you'll hear the shorthand: Indica for relaxing and couch-bound, Sativa for energetic and heady, Hybrid for somewhere in between.

It's a useful starting point and a genuinely imperfect science. Modern cannabis has been crossbred so thoroughly that the labels describe lineage more than reliable effects, and researchers increasingly point to terpenes — the aromatic compounds that also make lemons smell like lemons — as better predictors of how something will actually feel.

The practical takeaway: use the category as a rough filter, then trust your nose and your budtender. If a jar smells great to you, that's real information. And your own body's response beats any label on the shelf.

Is higher THC better?

No — and chasing the biggest number on the menu is the most common rookie mistake after over-dosing edibles.

THC percentage tells you potency, not quality, and definitely not enjoyment. A well-grown 18% flower with a rich terpene profile will very often be a better experience than a harsh, poorly-cured 30%. Plenty of experienced smokers deliberately buy in the high teens and low twenties.

If you're new, high-THC products mostly mean you need less of them — which is a budget argument, not a reason to start there.

What to say to your budtender

You don't need vocabulary. You need to answer roughly four things, and we'll take it from there:

  1. Have you used cannabis before? "Never" is a completely fine answer and changes everything we recommend.
  2. What are you hoping for? Sleep, pain, social ease, creativity, laughing at a movie, getting through a chore list — all valid, all point to different shelves.
  3. Do you want to smoke? If not, say so. There are plenty of options that don't involve your lungs.
  4. What's your budget? Tell us the real number. We stock grams under $10 for a reason and nobody here is upselling you.

Ask us anything. Ask us to repeat it. Ask us to write it on the bag. Ask the question you think is stupid — we promise it isn't, and we've heard it before.

The Alaska rules worth knowing

  • 21+ only, with valid ID, every time.
  • Never drive high. Alaska DUI law covers cannabis, and impaired is impaired. Plan your ride before your first dose, not after.
  • No public consumption. Not in the parking lot, not on the sidewalk, not in your parked car. Private property only.
  • Don't cross state lines with it — including flying out of Fairbanks. Cannabis is federally illegal, and airports are federal jurisdiction.
  • Never give it to a minor. Serious criminal offense, no exceptions.
  • Store it locked and out of reach of kids and pets. Edibles look exactly like candy because they basically are.

One more thing

Cannabis interacts with some medications and isn't right for everyone — particularly if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, have a heart condition, or have a personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia. We're budtenders, not doctors. If you're managing a health condition, talk to one.

Storing it in Fairbanks

Cannabis keeps best somewhere cool, dark, dry, and airtight — a sealed glass jar in a cupboard is close to ideal. Heat and light degrade it fastest, so the top of the fridge and a sunny windowsill are both bad ideas.

Two Interior Alaska specifics: don't leave it in your car at forty below, and don't put it in the freezer. Extreme cold makes the trichomes brittle enough to snap off, and repeated freeze-thaw cycles introduce moisture, which invites mold. Room temperature, sealed, in the dark. That's it.
