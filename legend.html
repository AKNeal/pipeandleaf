Menu Legend

Every number on the shelf, decoded.

Dispensary menus are written in a dialect nobody teaches you. Here's the whole vocabulary — percentages, weights, labels, and the words we shout across the counter — in language that assumes nothing.

How the flower is sold

Same plant, different packaging. The label tells you how it got into the bag.

Deli
Weighed out fresh in front of you, from a big jar, in whatever amount you want. You get to look at it and smell it first. The dispensary equivalent of a butcher counter — and the reason our shop smells like it does.
Pre-packed
Pre-packaged
Already weighed and sealed at a set amount. Faster, usually a little cheaper, no sniffing involved. Good when you know what you want.
Big Buds
Exactly what it says — the larger, denser, prettier buds pulled from the harvest. Premium selection, priced accordingly.
Bud Bar
Our value shelf. Solid everyday flower at the friendliest prices in the case.

Weights, and what they cost

Cannabis is sold in grams and in fractions of an ounce, which is a genuinely annoying mix of metric and imperial that everyone has just agreed to live with.

Buying bigger is cheaper per gram. A gram might run $13 on its own while the same flower works out closer to $10 a gram by the ounce. If you know you like it, size up. If you don't, don't — stale weed is a worse deal than expensive weed.

Alaska possession limit

Adults 21+ may possess up to one ounce of marijuana. That's the ceiling for what you can carry, so it's also the practical ceiling on a single trip.

The percentages

THC %
The compound that gets you high. On flower, typically 15–30%. On concentrates, 60–80%+. Higher means stronger per puff — it does not mean better. See below.
CBD %
Non-intoxicating. Won't get you high on its own, and is often sought for calm or physical discomfort. Most flower has trace amounts; some strains are bred for it specifically.
mg THC
How edibles, drinks, tinctures and capsules are measured — milligrams per package, and ideally per piece. A 100 mg bag of ten gummies is 10 mg each. Read which number you're looking at.
CBN / CBG
Minor cannabinoids you'll occasionally see broken out. CBN is commonly associated with sleepiness; CBG is earlier-stage in the research. Treat both as bonus information, not the main event.

The high-THC trap

Percentage measures potency, not quality and not enjoyment. Growing conditions, curing, freshness and terpene content matter more to how something actually feels. A great 19% will beat a mediocre 29% almost every time — ask anyone behind our counter.

Strain type labels

Indica Sativa Hybrid CBD

The traditional shorthand: indica for relaxed and heavy, sativa for alert and heady, hybrid for a blend. It's a reasonable first filter and an imperfect science — decades of crossbreeding mean these labels now describe family tree more than guaranteed effect.

Terpenes, the aromatic oils that also give citrus and pine their smell, are increasingly considered the better predictor. Which is a technical way of saying: smell the jar. If it appeals to you, that's genuine data.

Product categories

Flower
The bud itself, for grinding and smoking.
Pre-rolls
Already rolled. Joints (paper), blunts (tobacco-leaf-style wrap), and minis (smaller, great for trying things).
Infused pre-rolls
A pre-roll with concentrate added. Substantially stronger than a standard one — not a beginner purchase.
Vape pens
Cartridges screw onto a reusable battery. All-in-ones are self-contained and disposable. Pods fit specific systems.
Concentrates
Cannabis reduced to its potent extracts. Rosin is solventless, pressed with heat and pressure. Shatter, sugar, crumble are solvent-based, differing mainly in texture.
Edibles
Gummies, chocolates, baked goods, mints. Slow onset, long duration, easy to overdo.
Drinks
Infused beverages and mix-ins. Often faster-acting than solid edibles, still dosed in mg.
Wellness
Tinctures, capsules, patches, balms and creams. Topicals generally won't get you high — they work where you put them.
Gear
Pipes, bongs, papers, grinders, dab tools, batteries — and our glass selection, which people drive across town for.

Tags on our live menu

On the menu page, each product card carries a few quick markers:

  • A colored strain chip — indica, sativa, hybrid, or CBD.
  • THC % and CBD % where the lab results are available, or mg for edibles.
  • A Sale tag with the original price struck through, when something's discounted.
  • The weight or unit under the price — so "1 g" and "1/8 oz" are priced for different amounts. Always check which.
  • The brand, if it's from one of our Alaska producers.

The menu updates throughout the day from our live inventory, so what you see is genuinely what's in the case — but cannabis moves fast, and the last jar of something can walk out the door while you're driving over. Call ahead if you've got your heart set.
